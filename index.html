Hearing & Tinnitus

Browser-based auditory psychophysics and tinnitus phenotyping — a set of controlled, repeatable hearing and tinnitus tests that explore the relationship between peripheral hearing sensitivity, functional hearing and tinnitus perception. Intended for research, self-observation and hypothesis generation. It is not a diagnostic instrument and does not replace calibrated audiometry or clinical assessment.

The research question

When peripheral auditory input is reduced, why can the resulting tinnitus become a specific, audible percept — and how does it relate to the frequency region, magnitude and functional consequences of that loss?

One influential account of tinnitus links altered peripheral auditory input with compensatory changes in central auditory processing. That account leaves several questions a simple “hearing-loss” explanation struggles to resolve — and that measurement can address:

  • Does tinnitus pitch correspond to the frequencies with reduced sensitivity?
  • Does it sit at the edge of measurable loss, within the affected region, or at a systematically displaced frequency?
  • Can any displacement be expressed in octaves or cochlear-place coordinates?
  • How does tinnitus loudness relate to the listener’s own hearing threshold?
  • Does functional hearing — speech in noise — reveal information that pure-tone thresholds miss?
  • Can controlled external sounds produce reproducible changes in the percept?
  • Are these relationships consistent within a person when the measurements are repeated?

The panel is designed to collect the measurements needed to explore those questions — not to assume a mechanism.

What the panel measures

Hearing profile. Per-ear detection thresholds from 250 Hz–8 kHz, with optional exploratory extended-high-frequency measurements. Each threshold comes with an uncertainty estimate, a false-alarm rate, a response-quality flag and test–retest variation. These are self-referenced relative thresholds, not calibrated dB HL.

Functional hearing. A digits-in-noise test estimates the signal-to-noise ratio needed to recognise spoken digits. A listener can have relatively preserved thresholds yet still struggle to follow speech in noise, so this adds information pure-tone testing alone can miss.

Tinnitus phenotype. The percept is characterised by pitch (with an explicit octave check), loudness relative to threshold, sound character, minimum masking level and residual inhibition. These describe the percept; they do not determine its medical cause.

Where does tinnitus pitch sit relative to hearing loss?

A simple missing-input model might predict that tinnitus should correspond to the frequency region where auditory sensitivity is reduced. In practice, tinnitus pitch does not necessarily coincide with the audiometric edge. The panel therefore estimates these quantities together and compares them:

hearing profile → loss edge → tinnitus pitch → octave displacement → estimated tonotopic position

This gives a measurable way to ask a concrete question: does the tinnitus follow the missing auditory information, the boundary of that information, or a displaced frequency? The result is correlational and specific to the individual — it does not establish why any displacement occurs. This measurable relationship, rather than any single test, is the primary analytical question of the project.

Why does the percept become frequency-specific?

Auditory systems contain substantial spontaneous neural activity even in silence. A central question in tinnitus research is why a change in peripheral input can be associated with a consciously perceived, frequency-specific sound, rather than simply an imperceptible shift in background neural activity. The panel therefore measures the tinnitus percept alongside the frequency-dependent hearing profile, so the relationship between sensitivity, pitch and loudness can be examined behaviourally.

Mechanistic hypotheses

The measurements are compatible with several overlapping explanations, which may coexist. The panel does not determine which mechanism is responsible in an individual.

  • Peripheral change — altered cochlear function changes the input reaching the auditory pathway.
  • Central compensation — reduced or degraded input may drive adaptive changes in neural gain and spontaneous activity.
  • Internal-noise / stochastic-resonance models — changes in internal neural noise may alter the detectability of weak auditory signals.
  • Predictive-processing models — changes in sensory evidence may interact with the brain’s expectations about auditory input.

The experiments provide behavioural observations relevant to these models. They do not establish a mechanism or identify a treatment target. These are theoretical frameworks rather than competing diagnoses.

Controlled auditory perturbation

A secondary, explicitly experimental layer asks whether the tinnitus is sensitive to specific features of external sound. Stimuli can be varied in frequency, bandwidth, harmonic structure, spectral peaks, modulation, masking characteristics and controlled spectral degradation. The tinnitus is recorded before and after each stimulus, including a short recovery period.

The aim is not to reproduce a harmful exposure or “test tolerance”, but to determine whether particular acoustic features produce reproducible perceptual changes at comfortable levels. One later condition explores spectrally degraded, telephone-like signals — a hypothesis-generating experiment, not an assumption that codec artefacts cause tinnitus.

What this does not assume: the experiments do not assume that acoustic energy is stored in the middle ear, or that tinnitus is a mechanically resonating structure — passive mechanical energy is dissipated rapidly and does not provide an established explanation for a persistent tinnitus percept. Persistent tinnitus is treated as a perceptual phenomenon potentially involving peripheral and/or central mechanisms.

Measurement philosophy

A browser cannot measure the actual sound pressure at the eardrum — device, DAC, operating system, headphones and acoustic coupling all affect the delivered sound. Absolute levels here are therefore not clinical measurements. The panel deliberately prioritises what is meaningful on uncalibrated hardware: within-session relationships, repeated measurements, uncertainty, test–retest behaviour, frequency relationships, before/after changes and raw-data export. The objective is to make the limitations visible rather than hide them behind apparently precise numbers.

What this can — and cannot — establish

It can explore

  • an individual hearing profile
  • tinnitus pitch & loudness relationships
  • frequency-dependent patterns
  • functional hearing in noise
  • residual inhibition
  • stimulus reactivity
  • measurement repeatability

It cannot establish

  • the medical cause of tinnitus
  • cochlear pathology
  • calibrated hearing thresholds
  • a specific neural mechanism
  • treatment efficacy
  • a cure

Current scope

The present implementation is designed for exploratory, repeated single-subject measurements — one listener’s hearing profile alongside their own tinnitus. It is not validated for population-level inference or clinical decision-making, and a single result does not validate any mechanism.

Important limitations & safety

This is a browser-based research/phenotyping tool, not a clinical audiometer or diagnostic device. Browser audio cannot establish calibrated dB SPL or dB HL — output depends on the device, DAC, operating system, headphones and coupling — so results are useful for within-session relationships, trends and repeat measurements, not clinical threshold classification.

Do not increase levels to test tolerance or to reproduce a previous adverse sound exposure. Sudden hearing loss, new one-sided or asymmetric change, pulsatile tinnitus, significant vertigo, or other acute auditory/neurological symptoms require professional assessment — see an audiologist or ENT, not a web page.

A web browser cannot produce a known sound-pressure level: the actual level at the eardrum depends on the device, the DAC, the headphones and the OS volume, none of which the page can read. Measurement spine. 0 Signal & level foundations

Shared machinery every test is built on, via the Web Audio API.

Relative level model substrate

Measures
Sound level as attenuation below a user-set comfort ceiling, not absolute SPL.
Method
A tone/noise passes through a gain node; level in "relative dB" maps to gain by g = ceiling · 10(dB − 75)/20 over a 75 dB working range. The ceiling is fixed by a 1 kHz reference the user raises to "loud but comfortable".
Basis
Amplitude scaling: a change of N dB is a factor 10N/20 in amplitude (20·log₁₀). Correct as a relative scale; the absolute anchor is unknown.
Output
All thresholds/loudness are "dB relative" — later re-referenced to the listener's own best.
Limits
Not dB HL/SPL. Device- and headphone-dependent; only meaningful within one session on one setup.

Coloured-noise generators substrate

Measures
—
Method
White via a seeded LCG; pink (−3 dB/oct) by the Paul-Kellet IIR approximation; brown by leaky integration; blue by differencing; speech-shaped by band-passing pink. Used for maskers, therapy and the speech-in-noise masker.
Basis
Perceptually, pink/speech-shaped noise matches natural spectra far better than white, which is harsh and fatiguing.
Limits
Buffer approximations, not laboratory-exact spectra.

1 Pure-tone audiometry

A staged, per-ear threshold search across the standard audiometric frequencies (250 Hz–8 kHz), delivered as a numbered pipeline because the order matters.

1.1 Comfort-ceiling calibration setup

Measures
The loudest level the test is allowed to use.
Method
The listener raises a slider on a 1 kHz tone to "clearly audible, never uncomfortable"; that gain becomes the ceiling, and the 75 dB range sits below it.
Basis
A safe, self-set upper bound in place of a calibrated audiometer output.
Limits
Subjective; the true SPL of the ceiling is unknown, so it anchors a relative scale only.

1.2 Device / channel check verification

Measures
Stereo routing (L/R correct, not reversed or leaking) and the audio sample rate.
Method
A tone panned hard-left then hard-right; the listener names the side. Sample rate is read from the audio context and gates extended highs (Nyquist).
Basis
Per-ear testing is only valid with true stereo separation; a reversed or leaking channel would misattribute an ear.
Limits
Confirms routing, not acoustic isolation — no microphone means real inter-aural attenuation and headphone response are unmeasured.

1.3 Warbled-chirp stimulus stimulus

Method
Each presentation is 3 pulses (~220 ms on / 180 off) of a sine, frequency-modulated ±4% at 5 Hz (a warble), at a fixed level.
Basis
Pulsed/warble tones are standard in clinical audiometry: pulsing aids detection and, critically, distinguishes the probe from tinnitus; warble avoids standing-wave nulls. A fixed level per burst means a slow or late button press cannot inflate the threshold (unlike a rising-ramp method).

1.4 Bayesian adaptive threshold core measure

The heart of the audiometry — a probabilistic (ZEST-inspired) estimator, not a fixed staircase.

Measures
The detection threshold at each (ear, frequency) — and its uncertainty.
Method
Each track holds a posterior over threshold θ on a grid (0–75 dB, 2.5 dB). A logistic psychometric function P(hear | level, θ) (spread ≈6 dB, 3% lapse) updates the posterior after every yes/no. The next chirp is placed at the posterior mean; a tone finishes when the posterior SD is small (or a trial cap). New tones borrow an informed prior from the listener's age, neighbouring frequencies and the other ear.
Basis
Maximum-likelihood / Bayesian adaptive psychophysics (ZEST/QUEST family): posterior-mean placement is efficient and the informed prior exploits the known smoothness of threshold curves. It is ZEST-inspired — placement is at the mean, not by expected-information-gain (the Ψ method).
Output
Threshold (relative dB) plus a 95% credible interval and trial count per frequency — not a bare integer. Simulation: ~6 trials/frequency, ~1.8 dB mean error.
Limits
Relative, not dB HL; no contralateral masking, so a large asymmetry could reflect cross-hearing; posterior width depends on the assumed psychometric shape.

1.5 Shuffled interleave & catch trials bias control

Measures
Response bias / false-alarm rate, alongside the thresholds.
Method
Tones are presented in a randomised order across ear/frequency/level (never the same pitch twice running), with randomised silent gaps. ~12% of trials are silent catch trials; a "heard it" then is a false alarm.
Basis
Signal-detection theory: measuring hits without false alarms confounds sensitivity with a "say-yes" criterion. Randomisation defeats anticipation and rhythmic guessing.
Output
Per-ear false-alarm rate feeds the response-quality flag.

1.6 Extended high frequencies exploratory

Measures
Thresholds at 10 / 12.5 / 14 / 16 kHz (optional).
Basis
Extended-high-frequency thresholds can detect changes not captured by conventional audiometry, but their interpretation depends strongly on transducer calibration and measurement quality.
Limits
Most consumer headphones roll off sharply above ~6–8 kHz, so an apparent EHF loss often reflects the hardware, not the ear — shaded and labelled exploratory, and gated on sample rate.

1.7 The hearing profile (audiogram) visualisation

Method
Thresholds are self-referenced to the listener's own best result (best = 0), plotted with 95%-CI whiskers; red ○ right, blue × left; no-response frequencies break the line. Optional overlays: an illustrative age-related shape (ISO-7029-style) and an estimated tonotopic (Greenwood cochlear-place) axis x = log₁₀(f/165.4 + 0.88)/2.1 — a coordinate estimated from frequency, not a measured cochlear location.
Basis
Self-referencing gives a meaningful "relative to your own best" read without pretending to dB HL. The Greenwood function maps frequency to position along the basilar membrane — the tonotopic view the tinnitus question turns on.
Limits
Called a "hearing profile", not an audiogram; the age curve is shape-only, not a calibrated comparison.

1.8 Reliability & test–retest quality

Measures
How much to trust the run, and its repeatability.
Method
Response quality summarises false alarms + median credible-interval + trials per ear. Test–retest re-runs the thresholds and reports the mean absolute difference vs the first run, overlaying it dashed.
Basis
An instrument should report when it doesn't know; repeatability is more informative than any single number.
Limits
Good/Fair/Poor bands are product heuristics, not validated clinical cut-offs (hence "response quality" and "repeatability", not "reliability").

2 Speech-in-noise

A functional measure — understanding speech in noise — that pure-tone thresholds miss.

Digits-in-noise (adaptive) functional measure

Measures
The signal-to-noise ratio at which the listener can just report spoken digits — a speech-reception-threshold analogue.
Method
Three spoken digits (device speech voice) over speech-shaped noise. SNR follows a 1-up/1-down track (all-three-correct → harder); the noise amplitude is scaled by 10−SNR/20 (speech fixed). A larger 4 dB step shrinks to 2 dB after the first reversal; the threshold is the mean of the reversal SNRs (first discarded).
Basis
The Digits-in-Noise paradigm is calibration-free and correlates with pure-tone audiometry; SNR is 20·log₁₀(RMSspeech/RMSnoise), and scaling the noise is a validated "fix-speech" mixing method. It provides a functional speech-in-noise measure that can reveal difficulties not apparent from pure-tone thresholds alone.
Output
A relative SNR threshold (SRT-equivalent). Simulation: recovers a known SRT to ~0.8 dB.
Limits
The speech voice's RMS is unmeasured, so the SNR is relative (true = value + an unknown constant), and text-to-speech is not the validated recorded corpus.

3 Tinnitus psychoacoustics

Characterising the percept — pitch, quality, loudness, maskability — and phenotyping its subtype. These are measurements of the percept, not a diagnosis.

Pitch match psychoacoustic

Measures
The frequency that best matches the tinnitus.
Method
A slow sweep from 500 Hz (documented low bound) rising over ~60 s; the listener taps at the match. Repeated up to 3× and combined by geometric mean. Each match is then octave-confirmed on a live fine-tuner (oscilloscope + spectrum + ÷2/×2 jumps + A/B against silence).
Basis
Tinnitus pitch matching is notoriously noisy and can exhibit octave errors, so the test explicitly checks octave alternatives; best practice is multiple matches combined (a Bayesian-sequential idea) and explicit octave testing.
Output
Matched pitch (Hz) with the spread across passes as an uncertainty.
Limits
Inherently variable even in clinic; a probe tone can transiently alter the tinnitus during matching.

Sound-quality audition psychoacoustic

Measures
The perceptual character (pure tone / ringing / hiss / buzz / low roar / cricket).
Method
The listener auditions synthesised exemplars and picks the closest.
Basis
Tinnitus is often narrowband noise rather than a pure tone; character informs the phenotype and the masker choice.

Loudness match → sensation level psychoacoustic

Measures
How loud the tinnitus is relative to the listener's own threshold.
Method
A tone at the matched pitch is set to equal the tinnitus loudness; the result is expressed as dB SL (Sensation Level) = matched level − threshold at that pitch.
Basis
A raw level is meaningless uncalibrated; sensation level above threshold is the interpretable quantity, and tinnitus is typically only a few dB SL.
Limits
A loudness-match dB is not a loudness VAS; both are approximate on uncalibrated hardware.

Minimum masking level (MML) psychoacoustic

Measures
The lowest noise level that just covers the tinnitus.
Method
Narrowband noise around the pitch is raised until the tinnitus is no longer heard.
Basis
A standard tinnitus-assessment metric; also useful for setting up a masker.

Minimum residual-inhibition level (MRIL) psychoacoustic

Measures
The lowest stimulation level that produces temporary post-stimulus suppression (residual inhibition).
Method
Triangular-envelope narrowband bursts (0.5 s on / 0.4 off) around the pitch, with an adaptive level search; after each train the listener reports gone / quieter / same / louder.
Basis
Residual inhibition is a well-studied phenomenon; a triangular envelope gives comparable results with more comfort than hard-gated bursts.
Limits
RI is variable and can interact with pitch matching; a single run is exploratory.

Questionnaire & auditory symptom profile structured history

Measures
Laterality, pulsatility, somatic (jaw/neck) modulation, fluctuation/onset, and a middle-ear symptom cluster (flutter, fullness, sound-triggered, swallow/yawn).
Basis
These separate red-flag and pattern categories: pulsatile → possible vascular; unilateral → asymmetry check; somatic → musculoskeletal; the middle-ear cluster records a symptom pattern that may warrant middle-ear / somatosensory assessment. It records patterns; it does not identify a middle-ear pathology.
Output
Prominent flags (e.g. urgent if sudden onset + asymmetric profile) and an ENT/audiology steer.

Phenotype classifier rule-based

Method
Presents observed characteristics → a neutral phenotype descriptor → clinical flags, with any aetiological associations clearly hedged as "possible, not causes".
Basis
A phenomenological questionnaire cannot infer pathology; the literature (and NICE) caution against treating psychoacoustic tinnitus features as a clean map to a cause.
Limits
A measurement summary, explicitly not a diagnosis.

Impact index subjective burden

Measures
How much the tinnitus affects daily life (0–100).
Method
Ten items scored 0–10, averaged ×10, banded Negligible→Severe.
Basis
Psychoacoustic pitch/loudness correlate weakly with distress; a validated-style burden score captures what matters for management.
Limits
Original wording modelled on validated instruments (e.g. the Tinnitus Functional Index), not the copyrighted questionnaire itself — for care, a clinician should administer the validated form.

4 Mechanism-probing experiments

The scientifically interesting layer: relating the tinnitus to the hearing loss, and probing whether external sound changes it. All at comfortable levels only.

Pitch vs hearing-loss geometry derived

Measures
Where the tinnitus pitch sits relative to the loss.
Method
Computes the loss edge (first frequency ≥15 dB worse than best), the octave displacement log₂(pitch/edge), the high-frequency slope (dB/octave, 1 kHz→top), and both estimated tonotopic positions (Greenwood cochlear-place function — estimated from frequency, not measured on the cochlea).
Basis
Tests the tonotopic/central-gain account: the literature finds pitch usually sits within the loss and often >1 octave above the edge, not at it. So it names a leaning — pitch at the edge → Model A; pitch well above → an emergent/compensatory frequency (Models B/C).
Limits
Correlational and single-subject; suggestive, never confirmatory.

Frequency-response / residual-inhibition map experiment

Measures
Where, across the neighbourhood of the pitch, external sound quietens or worsens the tinnitus.
Method
Comfortable narrow bands from ~½× to 2× the pitch; after each, the listener reports the change.
Basis
Frequency-specific residual inhibition; suppression near the pitch supports an RI relationship and can guide a masker.
Limits
Order effects and RI carry-over between trials; comfortable levels only — never used to provoke.

Probe & masker (modulation) tool

Method
A live, tweakable sound — tone / narrowband / notched / pink / speech — with AM tremolo, FM warble, beating, notch width and a master low-pass, updated smoothly.
Basis
Lets the listener match the tinnitus character (pulsing, buzzing, warble) beyond pitch, and set up a comfortable masker; a notch removes energy at the pitch (experimental stimulus generation, not a validated therapy).

Prospective personal A/B comparison experiment

Measures
The immediate after-minus-before change in one outcome chosen in advance: tinnitus prominence, perceived loudness, or distress.
Method
Two Sound Lab conditions are compared over three balanced pairs (six periods). A computer-generated bit assigns AB or BA within each pair. Duration and digital gain remain fixed; later allocations and interim results stay concealed. A minimum gap and a user-confirmed settled state guard against immediate carryover.
Reporting
Condition means, usable observations, missing outcomes and early stops are shown descriptively after all six periods. The generated sequence and actual completion state remain in the raw export, following the transparency principles of CENT 2015.
Limits
The sounds are recognisable, so this is not blinded. Six observations cannot separate expectation, time trends, autocorrelation or longer carryover from a real condition effect. A recent tinnitus crossover trial found carryover despite a planned washout, illustrating this risk (Quemar et al., 2026).

Spectral stimulus reactivity experiment

Measures
Whether spectral structure — not loudness — changes the tinnitus.
Method
Controlled "degraded audio" conditions from a harmonic source: A full band → B telephone band (300–3400 Hz) → C band + harmonic peaks → D codec-like (band + peaks + soft distortion + a "boxy" speaker peak). Each is played comfortably; the app computes its spectral descriptors (centroid, flatness, crest, harmonic-to-noise ratio, peak list, a stored 48-bin spectrum) and records the tinnitus change before → after → a 5/15/30/60 s recovery curve. The listener's own recording can be loaded through the codec chain.
Basis
A behavioural test of the "frequency-selective excitation → nonlinear processing → persistent percept" hypothesis (H2→H3): does a spectral signature predict a change, rather than "did a tone help?"
Limits
Hypothesis-explorer, not proof; the codec→tinnitus chain is unestablished. Deliberately capped at comfortable levels — never reproduce a past exposure to re-trigger symptoms.

Sound-sensitivity & recovery log provocation-free

Measures
Self-reported sound pain/discomfort, tinnitus reactivity, trigger and recovery duration.
Method
No sound is played. Reporting pain or uncertainty activates a session interlock that removes the optional research sound stimuli.
Output
A structured log for export, not an autonomic measurement. HRV/pupillometry remain part of the separate native-iPhone integration plan and are not claimed by this browser.

Tinnitus spectrum, RI function & tuning curve research battery

Measures
A frequency-by-frequency tinnitus-likeness spectrum; residual-inhibition depth and duration by masker centre frequency; and the relative masker level needed to just cover the tinnitus.
Method
Randomised likeness ratings across tone/noise frequencies, comfortable 20-second narrow-band RI probes, and a multi-frequency masker adjustment task. The single-pitch match is retained separately.
Limits
Tinnitus matching and masking vary within a person; order/carry-over and uncalibrated transducers limit comparisons. These curves describe this session only.

Frequency discrimination spectral resolution

Measures
The smallest detected pitch difference around a 500 Hz, 1, 2 or 4 kHz reference.
Method
Two-interval forced choice: identify the higher tone. A 2-down/1-up adaptive track reports the geometric mean of the last reversals in cents and hertz.
Limits
Practice, audibility and the headphones affect the estimate; it is not a calibrated laboratory difference limen.

Gap & temporal-modulation detection temporal resolution

Measures
The shortest detected silent gap in a noise band, and the smallest detected amplitude-modulation depth at 4, 8, 16 or 32 Hz.
Method
Gap detection uses an adaptive gap/no-gap track with silent catch trials. Modulation detection uses two intervals (steady vs modulated noise) and a 2-down/1-up adaptive depth track.
Limits
Browser scheduling and transducers constrain precision. Human tinnitus studies of gap detection are mixed; neither result is a tinnitus detector.

Binaural lateralisation ILD / ITD

Measures
Headphone sensitivity to interaural level differences at 4 kHz and interaural time differences at 500 Hz.
Method
Two noise bursts carry equal-and-opposite cues; the listener reports left→right versus right→left. Separate 2-down/1-up tracks report dB ILD and microsecond ITD thresholds.
Limits
This is lateralisation inside headphones, not free-field localisation. Channel leakage, mono settings and headphone processing can invalidate it.

Dichotic stream attention selective attention

Measures
Accuracy while attending to one ear and ignoring a simultaneous competing tone stream in the other.
Method
Five separately routed tone pairs play; the listener counts high beeps only in the cued ear. The result reports overall, attend-left and attend-right accuracy.
Limits
A short tone-stream paradigm, not a validated dichotic-digits test. Ear differences can reflect audibility, routing, attention or chance and do not establish hemispheric dominance.

Mechanistic hypotheses the measurements can inform

The measurements are compatible with several overlapping explanations, which may coexist. The panel does not determine which mechanism is responsible in an individual — these experiments provide behavioural observations relevant to the models, not a way to select between them. They are theoretical frameworks, not competing diagnoses.

AMissing-signal compensation — the brain represents the deprived frequency more. Central.
BNoise amplification (stochastic resonance) — internal gain/noise rises to recover weak signals, and that noise becomes audible. Central.
CPrediction error — degraded evidence mismatches the brain's model, which fills in a percept. Central.
DPeripheral generator — the damaged cochlea itself produces abnormal activity (e.g. self-oscillating hair cells). Peripheral.

5 Reliability & data

Turning the tests into a defensible, re-analysable record.

Raw session export reproducibility

Method
A JSON export carries per-frequency thresholds with posterior SD and trial counts, catch-trial/false-alarm data, the hardware fingerprint, the full tinnitus profile, reactivity descriptors/spectra/recovery curves, and the raw trial histories from frequency, gap, modulation, binaural and dichotic research tests.
Basis
Exporting observations and posterior state (not just the final numbers) lets a session be re-analysed later with different estimators/priors, or compared against calibrated audiometry — the route from a clever app toward a validation study.

! Limitations & safety, in one place

  • Uncalibrated & relative. No true dB HL/SPL; results are device- and headphone-dependent and valid within one session/setup. Trust shapes and relationships, not absolute values.
  • No masking. There is no contralateral masking, so a marked left/right asymmetry could reflect cross-hearing rather than one ear.
  • Extended highs are hardware-limited. Apparent 10–16 kHz loss is often the headphones, not the ear.
  • Tinnitus outputs are measurements, not a diagnosis. Pitch/loudness/character are variable and weakly related to a cause or to distress; the classifier is a phenotype summary.
  • Comfortable levels only. The experiments never drive the ear hard, and must not be used to reproduce a past exposure or provoke symptoms.
  • Red flags go to a professional: sudden, one-sided, pulsatile, or asymmetric hearing change, or tinnitus with vertigo — see an audiologist or ENT, not a web page.
