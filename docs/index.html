Browser-based auditory psychophysics & tinnitus phenotyping

Where does the tinnitus sit, relative to the hearing loss?

A self-contained hearing & tinnitus instrument that runs in any browser — Bayesian adaptive audiometry, speech-in-noise, tinnitus psychoacoustics and exploratory mechanism experiments. Uncalibrated and relative: a research and phenotyping tool, not a diagnostic device.

The primary analytical question

Tinnitus is often discussed in terms of reduced input to the ear and compensatory changes in the central auditory system. A simple missing-input model might predict that the tinnitus should fall where hearing sensitivity is reduced — but in practice its pitch does not necessarily land at the audiometric edge. The panel estimates these quantities together and compares them:

hearing profile → loss edge → tinnitus pitch → octave displacement → estimated tonotopic position

Greenwood tonotopic coordinate is estimated from frequency — not a measured cochlear location. The result is correlational and specific to the individual.

What the panel measures

Five layers, from peripheral sensitivity to controlled perturbation and auditory-processing research tasks.

Hearing profile

Per-ear detection thresholds, 250 Hz–8 kHz (plus optional extended highs), each with an uncertainty estimate, false-alarm rate and test–retest variation.

Bayesian ZEST-inspired · warbled chirps · catch trials

Functional hearing

A digits-in-noise task estimates the signal-to-noise ratio needed to recognise speech — information pure-tone thresholds alone can miss.

1-up/1-down adaptive · relative SRT

Tinnitus phenotype

Pitch (with an explicit octave check), loudness relative to threshold, sound character, minimum masking level and residual inhibition.

geometric-mean pitch match · dB SL · MML / MRIL

Mechanism experiments

Exploratory: pitch-vs-loss geometry, tinnitus spectrum, residual-inhibition function, tuning curve, and spectral stimulus reactivity.

Greenwood place · likeness map · RI / tuning curves

Auditory processing

Frequency discrimination, gap and modulation detection, binaural ILD/ITD lateralisation, and dichotic stream attention—with a provocation-free sound-pain interlock.

adaptive 2AFC · temporal · binaural · dichotic

Built to be honest about its limits

A browser cannot measure the pressure at the eardrum — device, DAC, OS volume, headphones and coupling all affect the delivered sound. So the panel surfaces what is meaningful on uncalibrated hardware, and hides nothing behind apparently precise numbers.

✓ It can explore

  • an individual hearing profile
  • tinnitus pitch & loudness relationships
  • functional hearing in noise
  • residual inhibition & stimulus reactivity
  • spectral, temporal & binaural processing
  • measurement repeatability

✕ It cannot establish

  • the medical cause of tinnitus
  • cochlear pathology
  • calibrated hearing thresholds (dB HL)
  • a specific neural mechanism
  • treatment efficacy, or a cure
Current scope. Designed for exploratory, repeated single-subject measurements — not validated for population-level inference or clinical decision-making. Comfortable levels only; never raise levels to “test tolerance”. Sudden, one-sided, pulsatile or asymmetric hearing change — or tinnitus with vertigo — needs professional assessment.

Under the hood

Everything runs client-side on the Web Audio API. No account, no upload — audio is processed locally; only a numeric results summary is ever transmitted (and only if you configure email).

  • Bayesian adaptive thresholds (ZEST-inspired). A logistic psychometric posterior over each (ear, frequency), placed at the posterior mean, with informed priors from age, neighbouring frequencies and the other ear — reporting threshold + 95% credible interval + trial count.
  • Bias controls. Warbled pulsed chirps at a fixed level (reaction-time-proof), shuffled interleaving, and silent catch trials for a signal-detection false-alarm rate.
  • Speech-in-noise. Adaptive digits-in-noise with SNR = 20·log₁₀(RMS_speech / RMS_noise); a relative SRT from reversal means.
  • Tonotopy. A Greenwood place estimate x = log₁₀(f/165.4 + 0.88)/2.1 maps pitch and loss edge onto an estimated cochlear coordinate.
  • Spectral reactivity. Controlled degraded-audio conditions with spectral descriptors (centroid, flatness, crest, HNR) and a 0/5/15/30/60 s recovery curve.

Run it yourself

One self-contained index.html — open it locally, host it as a static file, or deploy to Netlify (the optional serverless function emails results via Microsoft Graph; set the MS_* env vars). No keys live in the repo.
